# Invitational data collection: open questions the 2026-11-07 event can answer

Status: draft. Written 2026-10-31. Companion to `review.md`, `phylax_design.md` ("Risks and open questions", "Internal questions to settle in the lab", T-matrix, "Scorer model") and the vault (`topics/quotient.md`, `topics/2027-preview.md`, `topics/environment-clues.md`, the MW Discord notes of 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-26).

## Purpose

The Invitational (4 hours, 2026-11-07) is the one realistic environment we get before regionals. A large part of our time there goes to instrumenting it and collecting data, not to winning. Every question below is something we currently guess, infer from a README or hand-transcribed packet table, or plan to "measure at the first event", and that can be answered by observing the live environment.

Ground rules for every collector:

- **Observe only.** Nothing here changes scored state: no config edits, no restarts, no reboots, no PCRs, no firewall changes, no extra accounts. Packet capture, log reads, `ss`/`systemctl show`/`Get-*` queries, file hashing and HTTP GETs are fine. Two flagged exceptions are actions we would take anyway (the T+0 admin rotation and one PCR, S13); they are timed, not added.
- **Known constraints.** Quotient and the inject/scoreboard portals are not reachable from inside the environment, so a person outside records the scoreboard (collector C7). No clipboard between consoles and outside, so scripts are fetched from the controller by short URL, and results are pushed or pulled over the network, never typed. No VM snapshots; reboots are costly. No AI tooling. Competition rules are out of scope (David handles them).
- **Time base.** Every record is UTC with the host's NTP offset noted (O5). The outside scribe uses the same clock.
- **Priority.** `must` = a design decision or a "never fail a scored check" safety rule cannot be settled without it. `should` = sharpens a decision or removes a guess. `nice` = useful, no decision hangs on it.
- Evidence ids: `D<n>`, `N<area>.<n>`, `A<area>.Q<n>` are `review.md` rows and questions; `T<nn>` are the design's test matrix.

1. Quotient configuration for the event: `Delay`, `Jitter`, `Timeout`, `Attempts`/`AttemptDelay`, SLA threshold and penalty, points per check, Divisor on or off, `ShowDebugToBlueTeam`, the per-check credlist mapping. (We measure S1, S2, S9 anyway; this lets us check our measurement.)
2. Whether `sysadmin` (or any default account) is among the scored users, and which credlists exist.
3. Which hosts serve the FTP, DNS and HTTPS checks, and which services are scored but not in the packet.
4. After the event: the Quotient per-round check log (round time, check, user, pass/fail, error) for our team, and the final score breakdown by service. This is the ground truth for S1, S5-S7, S11.
5. After the event: the red team's timeline, source addresses, tools and a list of implants placed before and during the event (the answer key for R1-R5 and T01).
6. The drop-flag timestamp (to the second) and the organizers' clock source.
7. Whether the Nov 14 session (and the regional) reuses this build, topology, Quotient config and red team approach, so that findings carry over.
8. The PAN-OS licence state and whether the Palo is a shared or per-team appliance (throughput and logging limits).
9. Whether the images are rebuilt from a template (so prebaked admins, "temp admins" and AD comments follow a pattern across events).
10. Whether the traffic generators / Orange Team exist at this event and what they do (services, volume, timing).
11. Whether the PCR automation question (A7.Q2) has an answer: if scripted PCR entry were acceptable, `pcr.submit` is revived; we expect not and plan for typed lists.
